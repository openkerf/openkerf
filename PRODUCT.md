# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Hobbyist laser owners who work like the author: people who own a laser at home or in a
small workshop. It is usually a CO₂ machine with a Ruida controller (the reference
machine is a 5030), driven by MeerK40t. They make their own designs and burn them on
their own material. They want to know the right speed and power before the material goes
in, not find out by wasting a sheet.

They prepare at a desk computer: drawing, laying out, assigning layers, checking the
estimate. They work at a tablet beside the machine: running the pre-flight, starting,
watching and stopping a job, and reading a test board. Both are first-class contexts.

## Product Purpose

OpenKerf is a browser interface and an API layer that sit beside the MeerK40t engine. You
draw or import a design on a bed drawn to scale and put shapes into layers that carry
laser settings (cut, engrave, raster, dots, with speed, power, passes and order). Then you
check one screen before the head moves. Success means a mistake is caught before the
material is in the machine, and the settings a user burns with came from evidence on
their own laser.

## Positioning

The engine already drives the machine. OpenKerf adds the bookkeeping around the numbers:

- a material library of presets measured on your own laser and your own material, each
  showing where it came from and how far to trust it;
- a test grid you burn, photograph and read settings off, with a QR code on the board so
  the photo finds its own row;
- a job you can inspect before anything moves: the pre-flight, the cut path step by step,
  and an estimate calculated from the real cut plan.

## Operating Context

- The engine runs headless with the OpenKerf API beside it. The interface is a SvelteKit
  static app reached over the local network. Native Mac operation is the current runtime
  (`Start OpenKerf.command`, `docs/running-on-mac.md`). Docker on a Linux box is the
  alternative.
- When the API is reachable from the network it asks for a token for write and machine
  actions. Until it has one, the app is read-only.
- The workflow is to set up the machine (wizard), pick a sheet and material, get a design
  on the bed, give it layers, run the pre-flight, estimate, burn.
- Other flows: test grids, generators (repeats, finger-jointed boxes, QR/barcodes, arc
  text, living hinges, focus test), tiling larger than the bed with two-point alignment,
  variable text from a CSV (one burn per row), and rotary.
- The laser physically cuts and burns. Wrong state shown on screen has material and
  safety consequences.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Information architecture is fixed by house rule (CLAUDE.md, "Where a new thing goes"):
  values go in the right context panel, verbs on the selection go in the right-click menu
  plus a shortcut, frequent verbs also go in the action bar, modes go in the tool rail,
  document- and machine-wide things go in the top bar, and searchable workspaces get
  their own window.
- Every action is defined once, in `frontend/src/lib/actions.ts`, with its name, shortcut
  and the reason it is unavailable. The menu, the action bar and the keyboard all read
  from there.
- English is the source language. Dutch is the first translation. Messages are whole
  sentences with semantic keys. Numbers and dates are formatted through `Intl`, because
  3,5 against 3.5 mm matters at a laser.
- API refusals are whole sentences with an error code, so the interface can say them in
  the reader's language.
- A route without a control on a screen is not a feature.
- The handbook (`docs/`) changes in the same commit as the screen it describes.
  Screenshots are generated by `frontend/gauntlet/`.
- Interface work never starts a job or moves the head to test something.
- Ruida connection repair and honest connection status are not yet validated on the
  physical controller.

## Brand Commitments

- The name is OpenKerf. It is MIT-licensed and positioned as the friendly front of
  MeerK40t, not a replacement for it.
- The voice is plain, exact and measured, as in the README and handbook: whole sentences,
  concrete numbers, no hype, and honest about limits. The product says what it cannot do
  and why.
- Status must be honest. A finished job must never look stalled, and a connection must
  never look healthier than it is.

## Product Principles

1. **Find the mistake before the material.** Pre-flight, estimate and cut path exist so
   the machine is the last place an error could show up.
2. **Numbers carry their provenance.** A setting shows where it came from and how far to
   trust it.
3. **Honest state over reassuring state.** Show what the machine and the job are really
   doing, including uncertainty.
4. **One rule, one place.** A behaviour that several surfaces show is decided once and
   read everywhere.
5. **Measure, do not read.** Claims in the interface and docs are backed by something
   measured.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA, full keyboard operation, and complete localisation (English source, Dutch
first). Tablet use beside the machine needs touch targets and legibility that work
standing up, at arm's length.
